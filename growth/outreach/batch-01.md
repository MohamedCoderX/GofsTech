## How to send

1. **Send from admin@gofstech.in**, not Gmail. Check that SPF, DKIM and DMARC are set up for gofstech.in before the first send.
2. **Send no more than 10–15 a day** while the inbox is new. Spread them through the working day in the prospect's time zone. Don't BCC or mail-merge in bulk; send each email one at a time.
3. **Personalise before you send.** Open the prospect's site again, confirm the observation is still true, and fix anything that has changed. If the issue has been fixed, change the line or skip the prospect.
4. **Plain text only.** Use at most one or two links per email and no tracking pixels. Don't attach the PDF to the first email; offer it, or send it when they reply.
5. **Follow up twice at most:** once after 4 days and once after 9 days, as a reply in the same thread. After that, stop.
6. **Log every send and reply** in a sheet with these columns: Date sent · Company · Country · Contact used · Offer · FU1 date · FU2 date · Reply (Y/N) · Outcome · Notes · Do-not-contact (Y/N).
7. **Stop immediately** if someone says no, unsubscribe or not interested, or asks you to stop. Mark them Do-not-contact and never email them again.
8. Only make claims that are true. Don't invent results, client numbers or testimonials. The only proof to cite is the four live projects: fitz1.in, reverserituals.in, aimaireinc.com and nancyequipments.in.

### Compliance notes (not legal advice; check before scaling up)

- **US (CAN-SPAM):** Use honest From and Subject lines and identify the email as coming from GOFS Tech. Include a **real postal address** in the signature (add GOFS Tech's business address under the signature for US sends). Include a clear opt-out (the "reply 'no'" line) and honour it within 10 business days.
- **UK / EU (PECR / UK GDPR):** B2B emails to corporate addresses can rely on **legitimate interest** if the email is relevant to their business. Keep a record of why you contacted them (the observation in this file), identify yourself, make opting out easy, and remove people at once when asked. Use generic role addresses (info@, sales@) where you can, which is what this batch does.
- **UAE:** Contact businesses only through addresses they published themselves. Identify the sender clearly, keep messages relevant, and give an easy opt-out.
- **Australia (Spam Act 2003):** Consent can be **inferred** when an address is conspicuously published and the message relates to that business's role. Every message must **identify the sender** and give a working **unsubscribe** (the "reply 'no'" line), which you must honour within 5 business days.
- **India:** No specific B2B cold-email statute applies, but the same rules apply anyway: honest, relevant, easy to opt out, and stop when asked. The DPDP Act covers personal data, so stick to business addresses.
