# Daily Routine, 90-Day Plan, and the Path to a Real Company

You are one founder who also writes the code. So the plan must leave time for client work.
The secret is not working more hours. It is **doing the same few things every day.**

---

## 1. Daily schedule (Monday to Friday)

Change the exact times to fit your life. Keep the **blocks** and their order.

| Time (IST) | Block | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 8:30 – 9:00 | **Plan** | Check WhatsApp/email/DMs. Reply to clients. Write today's top 3 tasks. |
| 9:00 – 12:30 | **Build (deep work)** | Client coding. Phone on silent. No Instagram. This is the most important block — it pays the bills and creates content. |
| 12:30 – 1:30 | Lunch + break | Rest. (On UK/UAE days, post at 1:30 pm.) |
| 1:30 – 3:00 | **Outreach** | Find 20 leads → qualify → send 20 personal messages. Record 3–5 Loom audits. Send due follow-ups. |
| 3:00 – 3:30 | Break / walk | |
| 3:30 – 6:00 | **Build** | Client work, testing, fixes. |
| 6:00 – 6:45 | **Content** | Edit today's reel or carousel (batch days: see below). Record a screen clip while coding — free content. |
| 7:30 – 7:50 | **Post + engage** | Post at 7:30 pm. 15-min engagement routine (comments on client + peer accounts). Reply to comments. |
| 8:00 – 8:30 | **Foreign follow-up** | US East is waking up. Reply to foreign leads. Send Upwork proposals. |
| 9:30 – 10:00 | **Learning** | 30 minutes: one skill (sales, AI tools, a framework, writing). Not random YouTube. |

**Total:** ~6 hours build, 1.5 hours outreach, ~1.5 hours content/engagement, 30 min learning.

### When a client project is very busy
Never drop outreach to zero. Minimum days: **10 messages + 1 post + 10 minutes of engagement.** Busy weeks end, and if you stop outreach you will have no work 4 weeks later.

### Weekend
| Day | What |
|---|---|
| **Saturday** | Half day of build or learning. Post as usual. |
| **Sunday morning (2 hours)** | **Batch content:** film 3–4 reels, write captions for the week. Then weekly review (below). |
| **Sunday afternoon** | Off. Rest is part of the plan. |

## 3. 90-day plan (27 Oct 2026 → 24 Jan 2027)

**Starting point:** 62 followers, 0 testimonials, 6 real projects, website being rebuilt.

These targets are realistic, not magic. Instagram growth for a small B2B service account is slow. **Most of your clients in the next 90 days will come from outreach and referrals, not Instagram.** Instagram's job is to make you look real and trustworthy when they check you.

### Month 1 (27 Oct – 25 Nov): Set up and start the habit
| Area | Target |
|---|---|
| Website | New gofstech.in live: pricing page + at least 3 case study pages |
| Instagram | New bio, highlights, 30 posts (calendar), daily stories |
| Testimonials | Message all 6 past clients. Goal: 2–3 real testimonials. |
| Profiles | Google Business Profile, Clutch profile, LinkedIn page, Upwork profile set up |
| Outreach | Inbox warmed up. ~300–400 personal messages total (start at 10/day, reach 20/day by week 3) |
| Calls | 3–6 |
| Clients | 0–1 signed (that's OK in month 1) |
| Followers | ~100–180 |

### Month 2 (26 Nov – 25 Dec): Volume and proof
| Area | Target |
|---|---|
| Instagram | 25–30 posts. Repeat the top topics from month 1. 2 reels with your face per week. |
| Outreach | ~400 messages (20/weekday). 50+ Loom audits. |
| Calls | 6–10 |
| Proposals | 4–6 (always 3 options) |
| Clients | 1–2 signed |
| Recurring | First care plan or AI-automation monthly client |
| Followers | ~180–300 |

### Month 3 (26 Dec – 24 Jan): Improve what works
| Area | Target |
|---|---|
| Instagram | 25–30 posts. One new case study reel per new project. |
| Outreach | ~400 messages. Drop the worst niche or channel; double the best. |
| Calls | 8–12 |

### 90-day totals (honest targets)
| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Feed posts | 80–90 |
| Personal outreach messages | 1,100–1,300 |
| Loom video audits | 120+ |
| Sales calls | 15–25 |
| Proposals sent | 10–15 |

**If after 90 days you have 0 clients:** the problem is usually the niche (no money / no need), the message (too generic), or the offer (unclear). Change one thing at a time and track it.

## 4. How big firms like Zoho and TCS get projects and get paid (simple version)

They did not get big from Instagram posts. They grew from **trust, focus and repeat business.**

| How they do it | What it means | Small GOFS version |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus on a niche** | TCS has teams for banks, retail, healthcare. Each team knows that industry deeply. | Become "the e-commerce + automation studio for D2C brands" and "the website people for equipment makers". |
| **Case studies** | They show "we did X for company Y, here's the result". Buyers trust proof. | One case study page per project on gofstech.in. |
| **Referrals and relationships** | Most big deals come from people who already know them, or from happy clients. | Ask every happy client: "Do you know anyone else who needs this?" Give a small thank-you (e.g. a free month of care plan). |
| **Retainers / long contracts** | Big companies pay IT firms every month for years (support, maintenance, new features). | Care plan ₹2,499/$79 per month, AI automation monthly fee. 10 care clients = steady income every month. |
| **Milestone billing** | Payment in parts when each stage is done. Less risk for both sides. | Your 40/40/20 model is exactly this. Keep it strict. |
| **RFPs (Request for Proposals)** | Big clients publish a document: "We need X, send your proposal". Firms compete. | Later: government portals (GeM in India) and company tenders. Too early now, but good to know. |
| **Partnerships** | Firms partner with software companies (e.g. a cloud or CRM provider) and get projects through them. | Partner with designers, marketing agencies, photographers, accountants who don't code. They bring clients, you build. Offer them 10% referral fee. |
| **Platforms and directories** | Buyers search Clutch, Upwork, GoodFirms for agencies. Reviews matter a lot. | Clutch + Upwork profiles. Get real reviews there. |
| **Products, not only services (the Zoho way)** | Zoho started in 1996 (then called AdventNet) building software tools for other companies. Over time it built its own products (CRM, mail, books) that thousands of businesses pay for every month. It grew without outside investors. | Turn repeated client work into a small product (see below). |

## 5. Path to a real company (1–2 years)

### Step 1 — Productised services (now)
"Productised" = a service with a **fixed name, fixed scope and fixed price**, like a product on a shelf.
You already have this: Launch, Business, E-commerce, Web app, AI automation, Care.
- Write exactly what's in each package on the website.
- Same process every time → faster delivery → more profit.
- Make templates: proposal, contract, onboarding form, launch checklist.

### Step 2 — Recurring revenue (months 1–12)
One-time projects = feast or famine. Monthly plans = stable.
- Offer the **care plan to every client** at launch (₹2,499 / $79 per month).
- Offer AI automation with its monthly fee (₹2,999 / $99 per month).
- Goal for year 1: **monthly recurring income that covers your basic costs** (e.g. 15–25 monthly clients). Then every project is extra.

### Step 3 — Register the business properly (months 1–6)
Talk to a local **CA (Chartered Accountant)** — this is worth the small fee.
| Item | What it is | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Business registration** | Start as sole proprietor or register an LLP / Private Limited company. A CA will tell you which fits. A registered company looks more serious to foreign clients. | Soon |
| **Current account** | A bank account in the business name | With registration |
| **GST registration** | Required when turnover crosses the limit (₹20 lakh for most services; check with your CA). Can register earlier voluntarily — some business clients prefer a GST invoice. | When needed / when clients ask |
| **Export of services** | Payments from foreign clients for services may be treated as "export". Ask your CA about **LUT (Letter of Undertaking)** so you can bill without GST, and about receiving payments properly (e.g. via bank, Wise, PayPal, Stripe, Razorpay international). | Before many foreign clients |
| **Invoicing** | Use Zoho Invoice / Zoho Books (free tier) or similar. Every invoice: number, date, your details, client details, GST (if registered), payment terms. | Now |
| **Simple contract** | Scope, price, payment 40/40/20, timeline, changes policy, code ownership after full payment | Every project |

### Step 4 — First helpers (months 4–12)
Don't hire salaried staff first. Start with **freelancers, paid per project.**
| First hire | Why |
|---|---|
| Freelance designer (Figma) | Better-looking work, saves your coding time |
| Freelance developer (MERN) | Take overflow work. Test with a small paid task first. |
| Part-time content/video editor | Edits your reels so you only need to film |

Rules: written task, fixed price, deadline, you review everything before the client sees it. Pay on time — good freelancers stay with people who pay on time.

### Step 5 — A small product from repeated work (months 6–24)
Watch for things you build **again and again** for different clients. That is your product idea.

| Idea | Built from | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| **WhatsApp enquiry + follow-up bot for small businesses** | Your AI automation projects (chatbots, lead follow-up) | Clinics, shops, service businesses — monthly subscription |
| **Voice stock manager on Telegram** | Your voice stock management automation | Small shops, warehouses, fabrication workshops — monthly subscription |
| **Simple quote & enquiry CRM for equipment makers** | Aimaire / Nancy Equipments-type businesses who get enquiries and send quotes | B2B manufacturers and suppliers |
| **Invoice automation tool** | Your invoice automation work | Freelancers, small agencies, shops |

How to start: build it for 1 paying client first → make it reusable → sell to 5 similar businesses → only then make it "self-serve". Keep it small and boring. That's how Zoho-style products start.

### Step 6 — Case study page per project (always)
Every finished project becomes one page on gofstech.in:
**Client → Problem → What we built → Tech → Screenshots → Real testimonial (if given) → Live link.**
Also one reel + one carousel per case study on Instagram. Over 1–2 years this becomes your strongest sales tool.

### A simple 2-year picture
| Time | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Month 3** | 2–5 new clients, 2–3 monthly clients, real testimonials, clear website |
| **Month 6** | Steady outreach habit, 1–2 freelancers, business registered, 5–10 monthly clients |
| **Month 12** | Known in 2 niches, 15–25 monthly clients, first referrals coming in, first small product being tested |
| **Month 24** | Small team (freelancers or first employees), product earning monthly, more foreign clients, ready for bigger contracts |

These are goals, not promises. The habit is what matters: **build well, show the work, talk to 20 businesses a day, and turn clients into monthly clients.**
